# SaaS Pricing Psychology Playbook

## The 3 Rules of SaaS Pricing

### Rule 1: You're Probably Charging Too Little
- Most indie hackers price at $9/mo out of fear
- $9/mo = you need 1,111 customers for $10K MRR
- $49/mo = you need 204 customers for $10K MRR
- $99/mo = you need 101 customers for $10K MRR
- **Higher prices attract better customers who churn less**

### Rule 2: Always Have 3 Tiers
```
Free → Starter ($29/mo) → Pro ($79/mo) → Enterprise ($199+/mo)
```
- Free tier: Lead generation. Limited usage. Forces upgrade.
- Starter: Solo users. Most signups land here.
- Pro: The "intended" tier. 60% of revenue comes from here.
- Enterprise: Custom pricing. Don't put a price — say "Contact us."

### Rule 3: Annual Plans = Cash Flow
- Offer 2 months free on annual: $79/mo → $790/year (saves $158)
- 30-40% of users will choose annual
- That's MONTHS of runway in advance
- Always show annual as default, monthly as secondary

## Price Anchoring Techniques

### The Decoy Effect
Show an obviously bad middle option to push people to Pro:
- Starter: $29/mo (5 projects, 1 user)
- Plus: $69/mo (10 projects, 3 users) ← Decoy
- Pro: $79/mo (Unlimited projects, unlimited users) ← Winner

### The "Enterprise" Anchor
Having a $499/mo enterprise tier makes $79/mo feel cheap.

## Pricing Page Copy That Converts

### ❌ Bad: "Choose your plan"
### ✅ Good: "Start free, upgrade when you're ready"

### ❌ Bad: "$79/month"
### ✅ Good: "$79/month — less than the cost of a junior dev's lunch"

### ❌ Bad: "Sign up"
### ✅ Good: "Start your 14-day free trial → No credit card required"

## When to Raise Prices
- If more than 5% of visitors convert → prices are too low
- If no one complains about pricing → prices are too low
- If enterprise customers sign up on the cheapest plan → you need better segmentation
- Raise prices 20% every 6 months. Grandfather existing users.

## The "10x Rule"
Your product should save customers 10x what they pay.
- If your tool saves a developer 5 hours/month...
- And a developer costs $75/hour...
- That's $375/month in savings
- Charging $79/month = obvious ROI
